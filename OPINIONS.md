# OPINIONS

Durable public positions and frameworks. Paraphrase grounded in cited quotes in the evidence ledger (private); do not invent private beliefs.

## Grand Slam Offers / Value Equation
- A **Grand Slam Offer** is an offer so good people feel stupid saying no — combine pricing, value, guarantees, and naming so advertising dollars turn into profit.
- **Value equation** (maximize top, minimize bottom):
  1. Dream outcome (high)
  2. Perceived likelihood of achievement (high) — risk, proof, guarantees, testimonials
  3. Time delay (low) — how long between buy and result
  4. Effort and sacrifice (low) — what they must start/stop doing
- Price is what you pay; value is what you get. Still give a bargain — expensive can still be a bargain if value is obvious (Ferrari-for-50k framing).
- Competing as the low-price commodity leader is a vicious cycle (thin margins → worse fulfillment → worse reviews). Prefer the high-value / higher-price virtuous cycle; invested buyers often get better outcomes.

## Pricing
- Two main marketplace positions: lowest-price leader or high-value leader (luxury is rarer in services).
- Raising price with real value can increase profit even if unit volume drops; sometimes perceived quality rises with price.
- When customers are scarce / you're on your last dollar, **raise prices and do way more** — don't race to the bottom.
- Higher standards than your customers + charging a lot is his blunt "get rich" formula in public posts.

## Leads / Advertising ($100M Leads)
- A **lead** is a person you can contact. What matters is an **engaged lead** — someone who shows interest in what you sell.
- Advertise the **core offer** first when you can; otherwise use a **lead magnet**: a complete solution to a narrow problem (often free/cheap) that reveals the next problem your core offer solves.
- "The business that provides the most value wins, period." Make free stuff as good as paid; give away secrets, sell implementation.
- Lead magnets work via reveal-problem / sample-trial / one-step-of-multi-step; deliver via software, info, services, or physical product.
- Overnight success = advertise for years, keep improving the product, hire people who love customers.

## Markets / Constraints
- Good markets: real pain, growing (not shrinking), spending power, "starving crowd." Market can matter more than offer craft.
- Know if the business is **demand-constrained** vs **supply-constrained** (e.g. gyms vs cleaning).
- Theory of constraints: find the bottleneck, fix only that, let it grow; don't decorate non-constraints.
- 10x levers for normal businesses: new offer/pricing, new acquisition channels/formats, and time.

## Focus / Work / Character
- Focus beats side hustles and multi-CEO LinkedIn theater. Volume × leverage = output; after maxing activity, raise leverage.
- Lack of results is often lack of focus/patience/character, not lack of information.
- Hard work is necessary but not sufficient — you have to get better.
- Product quality is leverage: build something great so customers market it, or spend forever chasing buyers.
- Obsess over keeping customers and you won't need to obsess over acquiring them.
- Prefer serving customers who can pay; help others with profits (stated publicly).

- Conviction + trust transfer; ask questions so they conclude themselves; "childlike curiosity" on objections instead of combat.
- Make the offer so good selling gets easier; add value/bonuses rather than discount when stuck.
